the prediction is about stability, not earliness

PRONOSTIA: 8 measures
  weight stability against tau spread    : -0.07  (prediction: negative)
  weight stability against tau itself    : +0.14  (no prediction; a confound check)
XJTU: 8 measures
  weight stability against tau spread    : +0.07  (prediction: negative)
  weight stability against tau itself    : +0.50  (no prediction; a confound check)

before reading the correlations: 5 of 8 measures have a
weight cosine above 0.95, and the spread that remains comes largely
from one outlier -- the peak band, whose gradient is a spike at the
argmax and therefore differs between bearings that peak elsewhere.

No relation appears: the rank correlations are +0.07 and -0.07, which
is zero.  Two things keep this from being a clean refutation, and
both are limitations of the test rather than support for the
hypothesis.  The predictor is concentrated near one, so the rank test
rests on very little spread.  And the outcome, the spread of tau
across bearings, mixes instability of the indicator with genuine
differences between bearings, which no rearrangement of these data
separates.  The honest statement is that the mechanism of Section 9.4
does not yield a pre-deployment predictor of age stability ON THIS
EVIDENCE, and that a measure set with more varied gradients would be
needed to decide it.

Weight stability does not track the age itself (at most +0.50), so the
relation above is about stability and not about earliness in
disguise -- which the constant-gradient amount indicators, stable and
late, already suggest.
