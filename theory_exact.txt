The increment is zero to machine precision in the residual form at every
eps, as the corollary requires: a channel the existing set can reproduce
contributes nothing, however large its own sensitivity.  The naive route
does not see this, and its error grows as the redundancy sharpens.  The
multi-channel estimator in this study inverts an ESTIMATED covariance, so
the same degeneracy is reachable from finite samples alone; the Wishart
inflation m/(m-K-1) corrects the bias of that inverse but not its
conditioning, which is a separate reason to prefer channels that are not
near-duplicates.

The 'grid' column is the study's earlier tabulation.  It agrees to the
fourth decimal everywhere except beta = 0.7, where it reads 0.4912
against the exact 0.5102.  The discrepancy is the grid, not the
formula: as beta approaches 1/2 the integrand tau^(2 beta - 2)
concentrates at the origin and a uniform grid mis-resolves it, whereas
the quadrature above reproduces the closed form to the precision on the
line above.  The earlier table is therefore correct wherever it is used
and imprecise only at its smallest exponent.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Design consequence.  Within the power-law family G accumulates as
tau^(2 beta - 1), so losing a factor 1/r of information moves the age at
which a target is met by the ratio r^(-1/(2 beta - 1)), which is small
even when 1/r is enormous.

  beta  information retained         lost by    age ratio
---------------------------------------------------------
   1.0              6.25e-02             16x       16.00x
   1.5              1.23e-02             81x        9.00x
   2.0              3.91e-03            256x        6.35x
   3.0              7.72e-04           1296x        4.19x
   5.0              1.00e-04          10000x        2.78x
   8.0              1.53e-05          65536x        2.09x
---------------------------------------------------------

That table invites the conclusion that a steep curve is forgiving, and
the conclusion is false.  The ratio is only defined while the reduced
demand still fits inside the record's budget, and a record carries one
budget: a demand set at a fraction q of it survives a loss of at most
1/q, whatever the curve looks like.  At the fraction of 0.35 this study
works at, no loss beyond about threefold is survivable at any age.  The
losses in the table are four to five orders of magnitude larger.

So the gap between an oracle and an estimator that must also fit the
trend does not show up as a later usable age.  It shows up as a demand
that cannot be met at all.  delay_measured.py confirms this directly on
the measured curves, which are not power laws: at a demand of 1e-2 a
hundredfold loss already leaves 48 of 117 records infeasible and pushes
the remainder to within 0.05 of the end of life.

========================================================================
RESULT 3  the nuisance factor separates from channel fusion
========================================================================

If K channels share the damage clock and the exponent but carry their
own amplitudes, D_j(tau) = A_j tau^beta, then profiling out all K + 1
trend parameters leaves

    r  =  1 / (16 beta^4),

independent of K and of the amplitudes.  Fusion multiplies the oracle
information by the channels it adds; the unknown trend divides it by a
factor that depends on the exponent alone, and the two act separately.
A practical curve is therefore the oracle curve scaled by one number.

  beta       K = 1       K = 2       K = 5      K = 20    1/(16 b^4)
--------------------------------------------------------------------
   0.7   2.603e-01   2.603e-01   2.603e-01   2.603e-01     2.603e-01
   1.0   6.250e-02   6.250e-02   6.250e-02   6.250e-02     6.250e-02
   2.0   3.906e-03   3.906e-03   3.906e-03   3.906e-03     3.906e-03
   3.0   7.716e-04   7.716e-04   7.716e-04   7.716e-04     7.716e-04
   5.0   1.000e-04   1.000e-04   1.000e-04   1.000e-04     1.000e-04
   8.0   1.526e-05   1.526e-05   1.526e-05   1.526e-05     1.526e-05
--------------------------------------------------------------------
largest relative departure from 1/(16 beta^4): 6.6e-12
The amplitudes were made unequal on purpose; the invariance is not an
artefact of symmetry.

Two further exact statements about the exponent term.  Setting the
logarithmic derivative of (2b-1)(2b+1)^3 / b^4 to zero gives
2/(2b-1) + 6/(2b+1) - 4/b = 0, which reduces to -4b + 4 = 0, so the cost
of an unknown exponent is greatest at exactly beta = 1, where
r_beta = 5/32 = 0.15625 (table: 0.15625).  As beta grows the
ratio tends to 1/2 from below (beta = 25 gives 0.4800), so an
unknown exponent never costs more than half the information, while an
unknown amplitude costs without bound.  A linear trend is thus the
hardest shape to distinguish from a shift of the clock.
