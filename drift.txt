The tail factor DOES drift, so the normalisation does not cancel it
and the ages this paper quotes carry a bias whose size is measured
below.

cells whose age moves by more than 0.05: 59 of 232
  25 amount, 34 distribution -- the correction acts on a minority of cells, which is why the
  median shift per cell is near zero while the family medians move.

The correction moves the gap by at most 0.056 of a lifetime.  The
direction is what settles the question Proposition 11 raises: a
correction that SHRANK the gap would mean part of the separation was an
artefact of the robust scale.

It does not shrink it.  On PRONOSTIA the gap is unchanged to within
0.008, and on XJTU-SY it GROWS by 0.056.  The applied
result therefore survives its own scale correction, and on the weaker
rig it is strengthened by it.

The drift is also family-dependent in a way worth stating on its own:
the amount indicators' residuals become markedly more impulsive late in
life while the distributional ones barely change.  That is consistent
with spalling, which produces impulsive excursions in level and leaves
the shape of the spectrum comparatively steady.
