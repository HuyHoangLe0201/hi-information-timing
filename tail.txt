A Gaussian residual gives kappa = 1 and kurtosis 3.  Everything above
one is information the robust scale credits to the record and the
estimator cannot collect.

checking the proposition against numbers already in the paper

battery : Section 5 gives 1.936 / 1.149 = 1.68 as the factor a
          non-robust scale removes; kappa measures 1.46 (ratio 0.86)
bearings: Section 6.6 measures the excess at zero offset as 1.35;
          kappa measures 1.35 (ratio 1.00)

The two checks are not of equal weight and should not be quoted as if
they were.  The bearings agreement is DEFINITIONAL: Section 6.6 measured
its factor as the standard deviation of the locally standardised
residual, which is what kappa is, so the two must coincide and their
agreement confirms only that no arithmetic slipped.  The battery is the
independent test: its 1.68 comes from a separate experiment that
substituted a non-robust scale and re-ran the attainment, and nothing in
that experiment computed kappa.

That independent prediction lands within 16 per cent, so the
two scattered corrections are one quantity, computable from the
record before any model is trained.

A caveat the identity itself supplies: kappa must be formed with the
scale the curve uses.  Taking a single global robust scale gives 3.20 on
the bearings against 1.35 locally, because it charges the tails with the
fourfold variation of the noise level over life as well.  The column
above shows both so the difference cannot be lost.
