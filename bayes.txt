fleet spread of life, and the prior it implies on the clock

fleet                   units  median life    spread      CV   s at tau=0.5
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
PRONOSTIA                  17         1428     12.2x    0.59          0.294
XJTU-SY                    15          161     60.4x    1.39          0.695
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
The tightest fleet is PRONOSTIA, coefficient of variation 0.59,
so the prior standard deviation at mid-life is s = 0.294 of a
lifetime.  It is used throughout because it makes the prior least
helpful; the looser fleet (XJTU-SY, s = 0.695) only strengthens what follows.

the demand the record must still supply, after the prior

A target epsilon needs G >= epsilon^-2 - s^-2 rather than G >= epsilon^-2.

  target eps   unbiased demand   after the prior   share removed
------------------------------------------------------------------
        0.30                11       none needed            100%
        0.20                25                13             46%
        0.10               100                88             12%
        0.05               400               388              3%
        0.02              2500              2488              0%
        0.01             10000              9988              0%
------------------------------------------------------------------
The crossover is at epsilon = s = 0.294: a demand looser than the
fleet's own spread is met at age zero, which the unbiased bound cannot
express since it asks for G >= epsilon^-2 regardless.

a shrinkage estimator against both floors

The estimator is delta_hat_MAP = G/(G + s^-2) * delta_hat_ML, the
posterior mean under the Gaussian prior.  It must sit above the
Bayesian floor everywhere; it may sit below the unbiased one.

MAP against the Bayesian floor: median 0.9999, range 0.9996 to 1.0011
Monte Carlo standard error on each ratio 0.0011, so the departures
are 0.1 standard errors and the floor is not merely
respected but ATTAINED: for a Gaussian prior and Gaussian noise the
posterior mean achieves the van Trees bound exactly, so unlike the
unbiased floor of Section 5 its tightness needs no measurement.

the unbiased floor is beaten at 6 of 7 information
levels, by up to 3.5 times, and only where the prior
carries a comparable share of the total information.

So Proposition 1 is not the floor a deployed model faces; it is the floor
a model faces that declines to use what the fleet already implies.  The
Bayesian floor is the one that covers every estimator, and it differs from
the unbiased floor by the additive constant s^-2 = 11.5, which is
negligible against the information these records carry late in life and
dominant early.
censoring efficiency, with and without the prior

  record carries G(1)   G(c)/G(1)   with prior   recovered
----------------------------------------------------------
                1e+01        0.50         0.77       +0.27
                1e+02        0.50         0.55       +0.05
                1e+03        0.50         0.51       +0.01
                1e+04        0.50         0.50       +0.00
----------------------------------------------------------
A record censored at half its information keeps half of it in the
unbiased account and more once the fleet prior is included, the
difference vanishing as the record's own information grows.  This is the
direction that matters for Section 10: truncation is less costly than the
unbiased form implies, which is consistent with the retention null found
there rather than in tension with it.
