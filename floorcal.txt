The two halves say different things and both matter.

On a record with no signal the diagnostic reads 0.67, not zero.
That is a property of the estimator rather than a bug: the floor is a
MEDIAN level subtracted from a density that is a squared quantity and
therefore heavy-tailed, so about half the samples survive the
subtraction and they carry most of the mass.  The consequence is that a
signal fraction cannot be read as the share of the density that is real.

Where there IS signal the estimate tracks the truth to +0.001, so the
quantity is informative above its floor even though it is not a
fraction.  The paper uses it as a threshold at 0.90, which sits well
above the 0.67 null, so the diagnostic as USED is unaffected;
what has to change is the description of what it measures.
